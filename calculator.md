Paid in 13 or 14 instalments depending on the CCNL: the 13th in December and, in commerce and services, the 14th in July. Or spread over 12.</span></div> <div class="fact"><b>INPS</b><span><strong>Employer social security.</strong> Pension, unemployment, sickness and maternity, roughly 29–32% of gross pay: close to Swedish employer contributions, about double the Norwegian rate. The employee pays a further ~9% out of gross; not an extra cost for you.</span></div> <div class="fact"><b>INAIL</b><span><strong>Workplace injury insurance.</strong> Mandatory, paid by the employer. Well under 1% for office jobs, higher for technical and production roles.</span></div> <div class="fact"><b>TFR</b><span><strong>Severance accrual.</strong> 6.91% of gross pay a year, owed to the employee when employment ends for any reason (Civil Code art. 2120). Every month we run the real payroll and invoice what it cost: overtime, bonuses, unpaid leave, sick days partly paid by INPS, raises and CCNL increases appear in that month. The payslip summary comes with every invoice.</p> <p>Billing: a refundable deposit of one month's estimated cost plus the first month in advance at the start; then each month paid in advance on the estimate and adjusted to the actual payroll on the next invoice.</p> </section> <section class="prose"> <h2 id="two-collective-agreements-compared">Two collective agreements side by side</h2> <p>On the same €50,000 RAL, the Metalmeccanico (engineering) agreement comes out roughly €1,500–2,000 a year dearer for the employer than Commerce and services (office roles). Contribution rates sit about 2–3 points higher, and technical work carries a higher INAIL premium. Commerce splits the RAL into 14 payments instead of 13, which lowers each payslip but not the yearly total.</p> <p>Unlike at home, you cannot choose to have no agreement, or pick the cheaper one: it follows the job and the sector. We name it in the contract draft and explain why.</p> </section> <section class="prose"> <h2 id="the-city-changes-net-pay-not-your-cost">The city changes net pay, not your cost</h2> <p>On top of national income tax (IRPEF), every employee pays a <strong>regional</strong> surcharge (from 1.23% up to about 3.33% of taxable income, depending on the region and income band) and a <strong>municipal</strong> one (usually 0–0.8%; 0.9% in Rome, 1% in Naples, up to 1.2% in Turin; many cities exempt lower incomes). Payroll withholds both from gross pay.</p> <p>Your cost stays the same; the employee's take-home does not. At €50,000 RAL the spread between the cheapest and the dearest region-and-city mix is roughly €1,000–1,500 of net pay a year.
