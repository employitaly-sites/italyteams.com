Payroll guide

From RAL to net pay

Nordic offers state a monthly salary. Italian offers state a yearly figure. Here is what sits between that figure and the employee's bank account.

The annual figure

An Italian offer states the RAL, gross annual pay. It is divided into 13 monthly instalments, or 14 under agreements such as Commerce and services: the 13th paid in December, the 14th in June or July. If you are used to "45,000 SEK a month", divide the RAL by 13 or 14, not by 12.

From each gross instalment the employer withholds:

  • the employee's social security share, around 9–10% of gross;
  • national income tax, IRPEF, progressive and reduced by employment tax credits;
  • the regional and municipal surcharges, which depend on where the employee lives (similar in spirit to Swedish municipal tax, but much smaller).

On top of gross pay the employer pays its own social security share (roughly 30%), INAIL insurance and the TFR accrual. See the cost calculator.

13th, 14th month and bonuses

  • 13th month (tredicesima): mandatory for all employees, paid in December.
  • 14th month (quattordicesima): only where the CCNL provides it, such as Commerce and services, usually June or July.
  • Performance and seniority pay: set by the CCNL, a company agreement or your policy.

All of these are taxable and carry contributions. The 13th and 14th accrue month by month, so a joiner or leaver receives the accrued part. In our quotes they are paid pro rata every month.

In 2026 productivity bonuses up to €5,000 a year are taxed at a flat 1% instead of normal rates.

Benefits in kind

  • Meal vouchers (buoni pasto): free of tax and contributions up to €10 a day if electronic, €4 if paper (2026). The Italian equivalent of a lunch benefit.
  • Company car for business and private use: taxed on a flat share of a standard cost that depends on emissions.
  • Health cover: contributions to supplementary health funds, often set by the CCNL, are exempt up to €3,615.20 a year.
  • Housing: rent paid or refunded by the employer, typical when someone relocates.
  • Fringe benefits (gift cards, utility bills, fuel): tax-free up to €1,000 a year, €2,000 with dependent children (2026).
  • Supplementary pension: TFR and extra contributions can go to a pension fund, the nearest thing to Nordic occupational pension.

Contributions and deductions

  • INPS funds pensions, unemployment, sickness, maternity and family allowances. Employee around 9–10% of gross; employer around 30%, by sector, size and contract type.
  • Ceiling: for employees first insured from 1996, pension contributions stop above €122,295 a year in 2026. The employee rate rises by 1 point on pay above €56,224 a year.
  • INAIL is paid by the employer only, at a rate set by the job's risk class.
  • Union dues are deducted for members who ask.
  • Pension fund contributions chosen by the employee.

Income tax (IRPEF)

IRPEF is progressive and withheld monthly, band by band:

Taxable incomeRate
Up to €28,00023%
€28,000 – €50,00033%
Above €50,00043%

Taxable income is gross pay minus the employee's social security share. Employment tax credits reduce the tax, mostly on lower salaries. Regional and municipal surcharges come on top.

State pension in short

The standard old-age pension starts at 67 with at least 20 years of contributions. Early retirement is possible at any age after 42 years and 10 months of contributions for men, one year less for women. From 2027 both requirements rise by one month, and by three months from 2028.
