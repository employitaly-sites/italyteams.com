# Unions in Italy for Nordic employers: less negotiation, more agreement

> Nordic companies are used to local union cooperation. In Italy, unions act mainly through national agreements. What a small foreign employer actually meets.

Nordic employers work with unions as a matter of course. In Sweden, wages are set through collective bargaining between unions and employers' organisations at sector and company level, and agreements cover close to 90% of employees ([Medlingsinstitutet](https://www.mi.se/app/uploads/the-swedish-model-and-collective-agreements.pdf)). Local negotiation and information duties are part of normal management, and Danish, Norwegian and Finnish companies have similar traditions.

So a Nordic company hiring in Italy often asks: will we have to negotiate with Italian unions? For a small team, the honest answer is: rarely directly, but always indirectly.

## Unions through the CCNL

Italian unions' main influence on a small employer is the national collective agreement (CCNL). Unions and employer associations negotiate it for each sector, and it sets pay floors, levels, hours, leave, notice and much more. When you apply the CCNL, you apply what the unions negotiated.

That is the indirect presence: every Italian employee's terms already reflect union bargaining, even if nobody in your team is a member.

## Membership

Italian employees are free to join a union, and the employer must respect that. Union dues are deducted through payroll for members who ask. You should not ask candidates or employees about membership, just as you would not at home.

## Workplace representation

In larger Italian workplaces, employees can elect workplace union representatives, with rights to information, meetings and paid time for union activity. Thresholds and procedures depend on the law and the CCNL. A small, distributed team employed through an EOR will normally not have workplace representation. As you grow, and especially if you open your own Italian company with a larger team, expect it.

## When unions appear in a small team

- **Disputes.** An employee who disagrees with a decision may seek support from a union, which can advise and represent them.
- **Settlements.** Agreed exits are often signed in a protected setting, which can include a union venue, so that the agreement is final.
- **Collective dismissals.** Procedures with union consultation apply above certain thresholds.
- **CCNL renewals.** When a CCNL is renewed, new pay tables apply to your employees automatically.

## Differences from Nordic practice

- **Less local bargaining.** In a small Italian team you will rarely negotiate locally the way a Swedish company does with a local union club.
- **Less information duty in daily management.** Nordic co-determination habits are good practice, but small Italian employers do not normally have the same formal consultation duties on everyday decisions.
- **More formal disputes.** When conflicts arise, they move to a formal setting faster than in a Nordic workplace.

## Good practice

Keep your Nordic instincts: transparency, early information, treating representatives as partners. They work well in Italy and build trust. Add the Italian formal frame: written decisions, correct procedures and the right CCNL.

As Employer of Record (EOR), we apply the CCNL and its renewals, handle union dues in payroll and manage any formal contact. You keep a direct, open relationship with your team.

## Questions we hear from Nordic companies

**Do we need to inform unions when we hire?**
No, not for individual hires in a small team. Hiring is notified to the labour authorities electronically, which we do as employer.

**Can an employee bring a union representative to a disciplinary meeting?**
The disciplinary procedure gives the employee the right to respond, and they can be assisted by a union representative. Plan for it and keep the procedure formal.

**What happens when a CCNL is renewed?**
New pay tables and terms apply from the dates in the agreement, sometimes with back pay. We apply them and show the effect on your cost before the invoice.

**Will Italian employees expect co-determination like in Sweden?**
Not as a legal process in a small team, but they will appreciate being informed early about changes that affect them. That habit costs nothing and pays back in trust.

## In short

For a small Nordic employer in Italy, unions are present mainly through the CCNL, which you apply to every employee. Workplace representation and local bargaining come with size. Respect membership, follow formal procedures in disputes, and apply CCNL renewals on time. Nordic habits of transparency and early information work well and build trust.
