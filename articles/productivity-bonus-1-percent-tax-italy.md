# Productivity bonuses in Italy: the 1% flat tax Nordic employers should know

> In 2026 Italian productivity bonuses up to €5,000 a year can be taxed at 1% under a qualifying company plan. How it works and how it differs from a normal bonus.

Nordic companies often pay a yearly bonus on company or team results. In Sweden or Denmark it is simply taxed as salary. In Italy, a bonus can be taxed very differently depending on how it is set up. A qualifying productivity bonus, *premio di risultato*, is taxed at a flat **1%** in 2026, on up to **€5,000** a year per employee, instead of normal income tax rates that run up to 43%.

That difference is large enough to shape how you design variable pay for your Italian team.

## What qualifies

The reduced rate is meant for bonuses linked to measurable improvements in productivity, profitability, quality, efficiency or innovation. In broad terms:

- the bonus must be based on a company or territorial agreement that defines the targets and how they are measured;
- the targets must be objective and verifiable, with a measurement period;
- the payment must depend on reaching an improvement, not just on the passage of time;
- the employee must be within the income limit set by law. The limit applied in 2025 was €80,000 of employment income; confirmation for 2026 was not found in our source.

A bonus paid at the manager's discretion, or a guaranteed bonus, does not qualify.

## Ordinary bonus vs productivity bonus

| | Ordinary bonus | Qualifying productivity bonus |
|---|---|---|
| Income tax | IRPEF at the marginal rate (23%, 33% or 43%) plus surcharges | 1% flat, up to €5,000 a year |
| Basis | Any policy, even discretionary | A written agreement with measurable targets |
| Eligibility | Every employee | Within the income limit |
| Employer contributions | Normal | Normal  |

For an employee in the 33% band, a €5,000 ordinary bonus loses about a third to income tax; the same amount as a qualifying productivity bonus loses €50.

## The welfare option

Under the same rules, the employee can often choose to receive part or all of the productivity bonus as welfare benefits instead of cash: health, education, care services and similar. These can receive even more favourable treatment . It is worth offering, because some employees value it.

## What this means for a Nordic company

**Design team or company targets, not only individual ones.** The Italian rules reward measurable collective improvement. That fits Nordic management style well.

**Put it in writing before the period starts.** A plan designed after the results are known will not qualify.

**Keep individual bonuses separate.** Commissions for sellers and discretionary rewards remain ordinary pay. That is fine; just do not mix them into the productivity plan.

**Mind the cap and the income limit.** Above €5,000, or above the income limit, normal taxation applies.
