# Non-compete clauses in Italy: why a Nordic template will not survive

> Italian non-competes are valid only if written, paid and limited in time, place and activity. What Nordic employers must change in their standard clause.

Nordic employment contracts often carry a standard restraint clause: a non-compete for senior staff, a non-solicitation of customers and colleagues, and confidentiality. In each Nordic country the clause is tested for reasonableness under local rules, and in some cases the employer must pay compensation during the restricted period.

Bring that clause to Italy unchanged and it will probably fail. Italian law does not just ask whether a restriction is reasonable. It lists conditions that must all be met. Miss one and the clause is void.

## The Italian conditions

### 1. Written form

The non-compete must be in writing: in the employment contract or a separate signed agreement. A handbook line or a verbal promise does not count.

### 2. A defined activity

The restriction must relate to what the employee actually does for you. It cannot prevent the person from working in their profession at all. "Any role in the industry" is likely void. Tie it to the products, services or functions where the employee holds sensitive knowledge.

### 3. A defined territory

State the area, and keep it reasonable. A worldwide ban for a mid-level seller who covered northern Italy invites a challenge. Match it to where you actually compete and where the employee had exposure.

### 4. A time limit set by law

The maximum is 3 years after the end of employment, 5 years for executives. If a clause runs longer, the duration is cut to the legal maximum. That is the only point where Italian law saves part of a clause.

### 5. A real payment

This is where Nordic templates usually fail. The employee must receive a specific payment for accepting the restriction, separate from salary, and proportionate to what they give up. A token sum is treated as no payment. A wider and longer restriction needs higher pay; there is no formula, and courts decide case by case.

The payment can be made during employment or after it ends, but it must be identifiable as the price of the non-compete. If it disappears into a general raise, expect a dispute.

## Loyalty during employment

While employed, the employee already owes a duty of loyalty: no work for a competitor, no use of company information against you. You do not need a non-compete for that period. The clause only matters after the job ends.

## Common mistakes

- **Copying the Nordic or group template.** A choice of Swedish or Danish law does not override Italian mandatory rules for someone working in Italy.
- **No separate payment.** Employment itself is not consideration.
- **Overbroad scope.** Industry-wide or worldwide bans.
- **Adding it later.** Agree it at hiring, with proper pay.
- **Assuming non-solicits are safe.** A broad ban on contacting customers can be treated as a non-compete and tested against the same rules.

## Do you need one?

For many roles, confidentiality, IP assignment and the duty of loyalty are enough. Use a non-compete where the person has genuinely sensitive customer or technical knowledge, and accept that it has a price.

## Steps

1. Decide whether you need the restriction at all.
2. Define activity, territory and duration narrowly, in writing.
3. Set a payment you can defend, and label it.
4. Have the final text reviewed under Italian law before signature.
5. Put the payment in the budget.

## Questions we hear from Nordic companies

**How much should the payment be?**
There is no formula. It must be proportionate to the restriction: wider scope, longer time and a larger territory need more. Ask us for the range used in similar roles before you fix the amount.

**Can we release the employee from the non-compete when they leave?**
Only if the clause and Italian rules allow it, and the conditions matter. An option to withdraw at will can make the clause void. Draft it carefully at the start.

## In short

An Italian non-compete must be written, paid, limited in time, territory and activity, and proportionate. A Nordic template rarely meets all five conditions. Decide whether you need it, draft it narrowly at hiring, price it honestly and budget the payment.
