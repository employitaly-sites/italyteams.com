# Italian Salary Components

> From RAL to net pay in Italy: 13th and 14th month, allowances, benefits in kind, INPS and INAIL contributions, income tax and local surcharges.


<section> <span class="eyebrow"><a href="payroll-guide.html">Payroll guide</a></span> <h1 id="from-ral-to-net-pay">From RAL to net pay</h1> <p class="lede">Nordic offers state a monthly salary. Italian offers state a yearly figure. Here is what sits between that figure and the employee's bank account.</p> </section> <section id="gross-net" class="prose"> <h2 id="the-annual-figure">The annual figure</h2> <p>An Italian offer states the <strong>RAL</strong>, gross annual pay. It is divided into 13 monthly instalments, or 14 under agreements such as Commerce and services: the 13th paid in December, the 14th in June or July. If you are used to "45,000 SEK a month", divide the RAL by 13 or 14, not by 12.</p> <p>From each gross instalment the employer withholds:</p> <ul> <li>the employee's social security share, around 9–10% of gross;</li> <li>national income tax, <strong>IRPEF</strong>, progressive and reduced by employment tax credits;</li> <li>the <strong>regional</strong> and <strong>municipal</strong> surcharges, which depend on where the employee lives (similar in spirit to Swedish municipal tax, but much smaller).</li> </ul> <p>On top of gross pay the employer pays its own social security share (roughly 30%), INAIL insurance and the TFR accrual. See the <a href="calculator.html">cost calculator</a>.</p>  </section> <section id="bonuses" class="prose"> <h2 id="13th-14th-month-and-bonuses">13th, 14th month and bonuses</h2> <ul> <li><strong>13th month (tredicesima):</strong> mandatory for all employees, paid in December.</li> <li><strong>14th month (quattordicesima):</strong> only where the CCNL provides it, such as Commerce and services, usually June or July.</li> <li><strong>Performance and seniority pay:</strong> set by the CCNL, a company agreement or your policy.</li> </ul> <p>All of these are taxable and carry contributions. The 13th and 14th accrue month by month, so a joiner or leaver receives the accrued part. In our quotes they are paid pro rata every month.</p> </section> <section id="pay" class="prose"> <h2 id="lines-on-the-payslip">Lines on the payslip</h2> <div class="tbl"><table> <thead><tr><th>Element</th><th>What it is</th></tr></thead> <tbody> <tr><td><b>Base salary</b> (minimo tabellare)</td><td>The CCNL minimum for the level</td></tr> <tr><td><b>Above-minimum pay</b> (superminimo)</td><td>What you agree on top of the minimum</td></tr> <tr><td><b>Seniority increments</b> (scatti di anzianità)</td><td>Automatic raises every few years, set by the CCNL</td></tr> <tr><td><b>Overtime</b></td><td>With CCNL surcharges, higher at night and on holidays</td></tr> <tr><td><b>Shift and night allowances</b></td><td>For night, weekend or rotating shifts</td></tr> <tr><td><b>Hazard pay</b></td><td>For roles with specific risks</td></tr> <tr><td><b>Commissions and variable pay</b></td><td>For sales and target roles; taxed as salary</td></tr> <tr><td><b>Performance bonus</b></td><td>Under a company agreement it can qualify for a reduced flat tax</td></tr> </tbody> </table></div> <p>In 2026 productivity bonuses up to €5,000 a year are taxed at a flat 1% instead of normal rates.</p> </section> <section id="benefits" class="prose"> <h2 id="benefits-in-kind">Benefits in kind</h2> <ul> <li><strong>Meal vouchers</strong> (buoni pasto): free of tax and contributions up to €10 a day if electronic, €4 if paper (2026). The Italian equivalent of a lunch benefit.</li> <li><strong>Company car</strong> for business and private use: taxed on a flat share of a standard cost that depends on emissions.</li> <li><strong>Health cover</strong>: contributions to supplementary health funds, often set by the CCNL, are exempt up to €3,615.20 a year.</li> <li><strong>Housing</strong>: rent paid or refunded by the employer, typical when someone relocates.</li> <li><strong>Fringe benefits</strong> (gift cards, utility bills, fuel): tax-free up to €1,000 a year, €2,000 with dependent children (2026).</li> <li><strong>Supplementary pension</strong>: TFR and extra contributions can go to a pension fund, the nearest thing to Nordic occupational pension.</li> </ul> </section> <section id="deductions" class="prose"> <h2 id="contributions-and-deductions">Contributions and deductions</h2> <ul> <li><strong>INPS</strong> funds pensions, unemployment, sickness, maternity and family allowances. Employee around 9–10% of gross; employer around 30%, by sector, size and contract type.</li> <li><strong>Ceiling:</strong> for employees first insured from 1996, pension contributions stop above €122,295 a year in 2026. The employee rate rises by 1 point on pay above €56,224 a year.</li> <li><strong>INAIL</strong> is paid by the employer only, at a rate set by the job's risk class.</li> <li><strong>Union dues</strong> are deducted for members who ask.</li> <li><strong>Pension fund</strong> contributions chosen by the employee.</li> </ul> </section> <section id="irpef" class="prose"> <h2 id="income-tax-irpef">Income tax (IRPEF)</h2> <p>IRPEF is progressive and withheld monthly, band by band:</p> <div class="tbl"><table> <thead><tr><th>Taxable income</th><th class="num">Rate</th></tr></thead> <tbody> <tr><td>Up to €28,000</td><td class="num">23%</td></tr> <tr><td>€28,000 – €50,000</td><td class="num">33%</td></tr> <tr><td>Above €50,000</td><td class="num">43%</td></tr> </tbody> </table></div> <p>Taxable income is gross pay minus the employee's social security share. Employment tax credits reduce the tax, mostly on lower salaries. Regional and municipal surcharges come on top.</p> </section> <section id="employer-breakdown" class="prose"> <h2 id="what-the-employer-contributions-cover">What the employer contributions cover</h2> <div class="tbl"><table> <thead><tr><th>Item</th><th>Covers</th></tr></thead> <tbody> <tr><td>Pension (IVS)</td><td>Old-age, disability and survivors' pensions; the largest share</td></tr> <tr><td>Unemployment (NASpI)</td><td>Unemployment benefit</td></tr> <tr><td>Sickness and maternity</td><td>INPS benefits during sick and maternity leave (not for all categories)</td></tr> <tr><td>Family allowances (CUAF)</td><td>Family support schemes</td></tr> <tr><td>TFR guarantee fund</td><td>Pays severance if an employer cannot</td></tr> <tr><td>Wage guarantee funds</td><td>Short-time work schemes, by sector and size</td></tr> <tr><td>INAIL</td><td>Workplace injury and occupational disease</td></tr> <tr><td>TFR accrual</td><td>6.91% set aside for severance (not a tax: it belongs to the employee)</td></tr> </tbody> </table></div>  </section> <section id="pension" class="prose"> <h2 id="state-pension-in-short">State pension in short</h2> <p>The standard old-age pension starts at 67 with at least 20 years of contributions. Early retirement is possible at any age after 42 years and 10 months of contributions for men, one year less for women. From 2027 both requirements rise by one month, and by three months from 2028.</p> </section> <section class="prose"><h2 id="more-in-the-guide">More in the guide</h2><ul> <li><a href="italy-eor-vs-entity.html">EOR, own company or freelancers?</a></li> <li><a href="italy-payroll-calendar.html">The monthly cycle and filings</a></li> <li><a href="italy-salary-components.html">From RAL to net pay</a></li> <li><a href="italy-employment-contracts.html">Contracts, leave and exits</a></li> <li><a href="italy-payroll-outsourcing.html">Outsourcing payroll</a></li> </ul></section> <div class="cta"><a class="btn" href="contact.html">Get a quote</a></div>
